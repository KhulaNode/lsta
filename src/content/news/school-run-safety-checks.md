Every school day begins with a journey. Across Limpopo, thousands of learners travel from their homes to schools using scholar transport services. For many families, these journeys are an essential part of daily life, connecting children to education and opportunity.

Behind every successful school run is a shared responsibility involving transport operators, drivers, parents, guardians and schools.

While vehicle safety and responsible driving are essential, **a safe and reliable school journey also depends on preparation, communication and consistent daily routines.**

The Limpopo Scholar Transport Alliance (LSTA) encourages greater awareness of these everyday practices and the important role each stakeholder plays in protecting learners.

## Safety Begins Before the Vehicle Moves

A responsible school transport routine starts long before the first learner is collected.

Drivers and operators should make time to check that their vehicles are in a suitable condition for the journey ahead.

Simple daily checks can help identify potential problems before they become serious safety concerns.

These include:

- **Tyres:** Checking for visible damage, unusual wear and appropriate inflation.
- **Lights and indicators:** Ensuring essential lighting and signalling equipment works correctly.
- **Brakes:** Paying attention to unusual sounds, changes in braking performance or warning indicators.
- **Windows and mirrors:** Maintaining clear visibility around the vehicle.
- **Doors and seatbelts:** Confirming that doors operate properly and fitted seatbelts are functional.
- **General vehicle condition:** Looking for visible leaks, loose components or other warning signs.

These basic observations complement, but do not replace, scheduled maintenance, professional inspections or legally required roadworthiness assessments.

Where a safety-critical defect is identified, the vehicle should not be used until the problem has been properly addressed.

## Establishing Consistent Collection and Drop-Off Routines

Predictability is especially important when transporting children.

Parents need to know when and where their children will be collected, while schools and operators benefit from clear arrangements that reduce confusion during busy morning and afternoon periods.

Operators should establish practical collection and drop-off procedures that are understood by everyone involved.

These may include agreed pickup locations, reasonable waiting arrangements and clear communication when schedules change.

Particular care should be taken when learners enter or leave vehicles near busy roads.

Drivers should avoid unsafe stopping locations and ensure that learners can board and exit without unnecessary exposure to moving traffic.

For younger learners, appropriate supervision and clear handover arrangements are especially important.

## Communication Builds Trust

One of the most common frustrations in daily transport arrangements is uncertainty.

A vehicle running late, a learner who will not be travelling or an unexpected route change can quickly create confusion if communication is poor.

Good communication does not require expensive technology. A reliable telephone number, agreed WhatsApp communication channel or clearly defined contact person can make a meaningful difference.

### Questions Every Transport Arrangement Should Address

**1. Who should parents contact when a vehicle is delayed?**

Families should know whom to contact for accurate information about collection times and unexpected delays.

**2. How will route or schedule changes be communicated?**

Operators should establish a consistent method for notifying affected families about changes.

**3. What happens when a learner is absent?**

Parents and operators should agree on a practical process for reporting absences so drivers are not left waiting unnecessarily.

**4. Who is authorised to collect a learner?**

Where appropriate, especially for younger children, families and operators should establish clear arrangements for collection and handover.

**5. What happens if a vehicle breaks down?**

Operators should have a reasonable contingency plan that prioritises learner safety, supervision and timely communication with parents or guardians.

Addressing these questions early can prevent misunderstandings and help build confidence between operators and the communities they serve.

## The Role of Parents and Guardians

Parents and guardians are important partners in maintaining safe scholar transport arrangements.

Their involvement can help ensure that learners are ready for collection, understand appropriate behaviour during journeys and know what to do when transport arrangements change.

Families can support safer transport by:

- Preparing learners for their agreed collection times.
- Teaching children to wait in safe locations away from moving traffic.
- Encouraging respectful behaviour inside vehicles.
- Reminding learners not to distract drivers.
- Reporting transport-related safety concerns promptly.
- Keeping emergency contact information up to date.

Parents should also feel comfortable asking operators about their transport arrangements, vehicle condition and applicable operating requirements.

Open communication benefits everyone.

## Schools Have an Important Role Too

Schools are often the busiest points in the scholar transport journey.

During morning arrivals and afternoon departures, several vehicles, pedestrians and learners may share limited road space.

Where practical, schools and transport operators can work together to identify safer collection areas, communicate dismissal arrangements and reduce congestion around entrances.

Clear procedures can be particularly valuable during examinations, school events, early closures and other changes to normal school schedules.

Cooperation between schools and operators can contribute to a more organised and predictable transport environment.

## Professionalism Goes Beyond Driving

A scholar transport operator provides more than a vehicle and a driver.

The service involves punctuality, communication, appropriate conduct, vehicle maintenance, responsible administration and the wellbeing of learners.

Professionalism also means understanding and complying with applicable legal requirements, including those relating to vehicles, drivers and transport operations.

Operators should consult the relevant transport authorities and official government resources for current requirements rather than relying exclusively on informal advice.

A culture of professionalism strengthens confidence in individual operators and contributes to the reputation of the wider scholar transport sector.

## Working Together for Safer Journeys

Improving scholar transport safety is not the responsibility of one person or organisation alone.

It requires cooperation between drivers, operators, parents, schools, communities and the relevant authorities.

Small improvements in daily routines can make a meaningful difference. A completed vehicle check, a timely message about a delay or a clearly agreed collection point may seem simple, but each contributes to a more dependable transport experience.

The Limpopo Scholar Transport Alliance supports greater awareness, responsible practices and constructive engagement within the scholar transport community.

By sharing information and encouraging cooperation, we can work towards a transport environment where safety, reliability and learner wellbeing remain central priorities.

**Every safer journey begins with preparation, responsibility and communication.**
